SHIPS · Social Healing Impact Peer Support

What if our adat & budaya already knew how to heal us?

For many Southeast Asian cultures, generational trauma leaves marks. Modern urbanised life isolates us from community, family and human protection.

People move away from hometowns, work becomes more isolating, and care becomes something an individual is expected to manage alone. Hyper-individualism has become the new rooted practice.

We are told to find who we truly are. But modern mental-health language doesn’t always answer deeper questions like:

  • Where do I belong?
  • Who am I connected to?
  • What gives life meaning?

We were taught to become more independent. Handle it ourselves. Set boundaries. Move away. Keep going. But humans were never meant to heal completely alone.

Modern mental health language helps us name what we feel: anxiety, depression, PTSD, neurodivergence, autism, ADHD, burnout. But naming something is not always the same as feeling whole again.

Have we ever thought that healing is rooted back in our own Nusantara culture?

  • Food
  • Craft
  • Music
  • Storytelling
  • Spirituality
  • Ritual
  • Community

Community ritual, art, crafts, peer support presence and ancient wisdom is how we reverse genetic healing.

  • “I belong somewhere.”
  • “I come from somewhere.”
  • “I am not carrying this alone.”

Going back to our roots does not mean rejecting therapy, psychology or modern medicine. It can mean bringing them together.

This Mental Health Awareness Month, SHIPS is opening the conversation through the #back2roots theme. Join our Art of Healing ’26 together with Suritma Collective at Mika Coffee, Ara Damansara.

Gotong-Royong Weaving Community Art

There’s something about weaving that mirrors how we heal. In Malay culture, gotong-royong isn’t just about getting things done together — it’s about showing up side by side. When your hands are busy and the person next to you is weaving their own thread, something shifts. The silence becomes safe. The space becomes shared.

There’s psychology behind it: repetitive, rhythmic motion (like weaving) regulates the nervous system. It lowers the guard. And when the guard drops, real conversations begin.

That’s the beauty of communal art. You start with a thread. You leave with a story — and maybe a new understanding of someone else’s.

by Diced Out

Remember congkak? Before screens. Before notifications. Just you, the board, and the quiet rhythm of moving one marble at a time.

Congkak teaches us something about strategy and patience: you can’t rush it. One move at a time. One drop at a time. Trusting the journey, even when you can’t see the end.

3:00 PM – 3:30 PM

ASWARA Art of Healing Performance

Pusat Pengajian Asasi Seni Kreatif, ASWARA

The body remembers what words cannot say. Kembali ke Akar — Back to Roots.

15 foundation students from ASWARA present a 15-minute expressive movement performance born from a Creative Expression Therapy workshop exploring the psychology behind movement healing.

We asked: how do Nusantara art and movement help us express, regulate, and carry emotion forward?

Through abstract and literal movement exercises, these students translated complex emotions into physical storytelling — not to perform perfection, but to show what it means to move through pain, joy, grief, and release. Where art meets the body. Where the body meets healing.

3:30 PM – 4:00 PM

Open Mic: Pass the Mic!

Before social media, there were the pantun and syair — much like what we do now with acoustic singing and poetry. These aren’t just performances; they are our oldest forms of expression. Long before TikTok and Instagram Reels, our ancestors passed down stories through voice, rhythm, and a live audience.

#back2roots means returning to that raw, unfiltered exchange — where you speak and the room feels you.

Bring your voice. Bring your words. Bring your instrument. Poetry. Acoustic. Spoken word. No judgments. Just witness.
